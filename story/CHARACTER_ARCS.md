# Character arcs and relationships

**Status:** Working audiobook fiction selected for drafting, 17 November 2026. Brunti's game identity and the identities of source encounter figures are established; personal histories, newly assigned names, relationships, private motives and outcomes below are **original adaptations**, not changes to world canon. Refer to [story spine](STORY_SPINE.md).

## Core company

### Brunti — the protector who must learn to ask

**Source fact:** Female Grimnir warrior, portrait 104. **Fictional role:** central viewpoint; young craft-trained warrior from a moving Skyland. **Voice:** direct and sparing, especially when afraid; her jokes emerge dryly from literal observations rather than grand bravado. She notices weight, sound, tension in joined material, workmanship and the different ways surfaces carry a touch.

Her strength is that she acts. Her weakness is that she acts **before** knowing what kind of help has been requested. She begins wanting to repair the memorial wall in exactly its former place and to prove that her first failure was temporary. She is uneasy with the idea that a correct answer might be incomplete. Her outward competence makes her bad at admitting injury.

**Decisions that reveal her:**
- Ch. 1: Makes a rash brace, removes it when harmful, and tells the truth about her part in the disruption.
- Ch. 4: Treats seven carved letters as if their meaning must be hers to discover; Sira resists that claim.
- Ch. 10: Opens a refuge for someone else's need rather than securing a trophy or shortening the road.
- Ch. 15: Stops interrogating a curious pool when a hungry child needs attention nearby.
- Ch. 20: Accepts that Jorvald cannot supply the missing portion of another person's account.
- Ch. 21–23: Chooses to help find displaced people even with a home deadline approaching.
- Ch. 25: **Relapses:** enters Blackwood chasing confirmation after the practical rescue is done.
- Ch. 29: Accepts the safe transfer, repairs what she harmed, refuses to present her abyssal impressions as proof.

**Physical note:** Her fingertips register vibration through crystal and metal. Cold, mineral depletion, fractures and forced resonance have Grimnir-specific consequences. Avoid sweating, bleeding or human appetite as defaults; consult [Grimnir life and care](https://github.com/babunidev/MorkahjaWorld/blob/f56a7c3d2034652e1c5961f0ece9c3ef52b52b48/Traditions/Grimnir_Life_and_Care.md).

### Darrik — the keeper of safe structures

**Original character; Grimnir, senior anchor-craft steward in Brunti's home Skyland.** Darrik is a builder who has tended inhabited chambers long enough to know what a beautiful failure can cost. His hands bear mineral seams burnished smooth by decades of work. He values memory stones; he distrusts attaching an uncertain resonance to rooms where people heal or rest.

He is **not a censor, villain, parent by default, or embodiment of an entire conservative faction**. His proposal is a responsible one: isolate the troublesome panel, then move and preserve it in an archive while restoring a safe wall for daily life. He clashes with Brunti because she imagines a transfer as a defeat and mistakes his caution for indifference.

**Arc:** early ch. 1–2, later messages through the road, then ch. 29–30. His error is smaller but real: he underestimates the value of making a preserved archive **accessible and regularly heard**, rather than merely safe on a shelf. At the end, he keeps his safety standard and accepts a new listening space as part of it.

**Bond with Brunti:** professional trust strained by her rash workmanship; reconciliation through shared, testable work, not an apology standing in for a repair.

### Sira — the Bloomwalker who refuses to turn people into examples

**Source root:** encounter **20186, Melody's Bloomwalker** — a young Verdani traveler in a deep-blue robe, accompanied by flower, song and water-like magic. **The personal name “Sira,” her history and her choice to accompany Brunti are original novel fiction; “Melody” is not canonically another person's name.**

Sira is traveling toward Aetherion and Evergrove carrying spoken messages from small communities whose words do not always survive formal recordkeeping. She sings in short fragments, often without claiming authorship, and knows that memory changes with each voice. She does not always heal people, nor is she a moral teacher who exists only for Brunti's growth: when frightened, she can lose patience, resent delays, and avoid telling uncomfortable truths.

She finds Brunti's confidence useful and occasionally exhausting. At first Sira corrects Brunti's habit of translating strangers into problems to be solved. Later she risks making her own mistake by assuming that careful listening guarantees she understands those strangers. In Evergrove, the damaged village gives her obligations independent of Brunti.

**Arc:** meet in ch. 4; joint road through ch. 12; personal duty to displaced communities in ch. 13; journeys through ch. 15; separated while making contacts in ch. 16–20, with reports delivered by ordinary routes; reunite near Keldor in ch. 21 when news arrives of a missing displaced child; help bring the child and other survivors to safety in ch. 23. She opposes Brunti's needless Blackwood venture in ch. 24–25 and remains outside to help those still living. She can care about Brunti without calling the venture brave.

**Thematic line:** “A song isn't a cage for the person who first sang it.” This is **original conceptual dialogue**, not a quote from game lore and not necessarily a manuscript line.

### Velen — the archivist who must leave the shelves

**Original character; Aevis copyist and junior archivist working with Aetherion's Archive of Echoes.** Their work involves attribution, damaged records and preserving an author's own cadence. They are careful with names and hesitant to offer conclusions beyond evidence. Their home background is not yet established as Celesterra by source evidence; any Aevis family history will be novel-only.

At first Velen trusts written records over living testimonies because people can misremember. Travel exposes the weakness of that certainty: sometimes an incomplete book repeats its error more confidently than a candid witness. In Everwinter, the fragments of the Shattered Seer and the scholar Jorvald challenge Velen's assumption that gaps can be restored by elegant reconstruction.

**Arc:** meet at the Archive in ch. 11 and find evidence of a composite memorial wall. Take part in research and journeys through ch. 20. Follow Brunti and Sira as far as Keldor's safer outer routes, taking careful notes about the missing child and aid; do **not** join the Blackwood descent. Velen's choice to document witnesses and keep a route open is real courage, not passive cowardice. Their records later corroborate the old repair diagram but never certify Brunti's memories of the abyss.

**Relationship:** professional friction and growing friendship with Brunti. Velen needs her craft insight; she needs their discipline in source attribution. Sira refuses to let either reduce a person to a data point.

## The two absent people at the heart of the stone

### Tavra — deceased Grimnir Skykeeper

**Entirely original personal identity and past.** A traveling bearer of accounts whose memory stone became part of the communal wall. Her echo contains a distinct rhythmic contour, not usable fresh testimony or instructions to Brunti. The Aetherion archive diagram suggests two beds without naming either; direct inspection of the old Skyland panel later identifies Tavra's stone.

### Korrum — deceased Grimnir builder

**Entirely original personal identity and past.** An architect of inhabited Skyland spaces whose memorial lies beside Tavra's under a shared casing. The second inscription was obscured during an old repair. Korrum's echo differs from Tavra's; neither is inferior or false simply because their rhythms overlap.

The two are **not revealed as divine emissaries, parents of Brunti, ancient creators of Keldor, or the speakers of all encountered stories**. Their names make the resolution intimate, not cosmological.

## Significant established encounter figures

- **Earthsinger (20185):** helps Brunti discover that answering a sound is not the same as controlling it; no invented secret ancestry or prophecy.
- **Joyful Riverkeeper (20193):** humor and continuity; counterweight to ceremonial seriousness.
- **Frostbite Fergus (20132):** a generous ice troll, memorable partly because he is hungry and friendly; not a hidden guardian of the plot.
- **Jorvald (20144):** a cautious Frostweaver scholar; offers methodological warning, not a magical Grimnir repair key.
- **Kaldric (20133), Lyria (20139), Shattered Seer (20137):** separate perspectives on loss and ruined knowledge. Their relationships and ultimate history remain unspecified.
- **Sageglow scholar, Skyshard Guardian, the escapee and lost Verdani young (20162, 20167, 20169–20171):** specific, distinct lives persisting under Keldor's conditions; don't merge them into a single composite survivor.
- **Unnamed Verdani healer in Verdani's Vigil (19672):** independently competent and skeptical about a patient's confused account; not Brunti's follower or a proof of what Blackwood truly is.

## Relationship conflicts to play, not explain

| Pair | What they each want | Where pressure changes them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brunti / Darrik | Restored hearing versus safe building | Her return accepts transfer; his restoration makes hearing possible |
| Brunti / Sira | A useful answer versus attention to the person speaking | Shelter, hunger, Evergrove's displaced people, Blackwood disagreement |
| Brunti / Velen | Craft inference versus documented provenance | Aetherion archive, Nekalos fragments, conflicting Blackwood account |
| Sira / Velen | Lived memory versus written reference | They learn neither form is automatically more truthful |
| Brunti / the absent two | Desire for one recoverable signal versus irreducibly separate lives | Archive drawing and final memorial re-housing |

**Cast discipline:** no chosen romance, destiny, invented master villain or additional divine figure. New surnames, private biographies and spoken lines enter only as clearly novel-authored material.
