## Editorial decision and boundaries

The recurring issue was not that Brunti often records what she sees; that is central to the novel. The problem was the regular explanatory loop **strange occurrence → possible link to the Skyland → denial of proof → notebook note → announced lesson**, sometimes repeated across successive encounters and in chapter closings. The revised prose preserves the essential notebook when it has concrete function: a source distinction, a visible route limit, an absent page, a Blackwood memory gap or evidence for Darrik. It removes or varies redundant instances when the event already demonstrates the limit.

Editorial defaults in this pass:

- **Play and ordinary life** may matter without delivering a craft lesson or clue. Preserve young animals, meals, market voices, inconvenient travelers, bees, the warmth of Aksa's hearth and Sira's humor.
- **Distinct sources stay distinct.** A traveler telling a rumor is not an observed monster. A distant archive description is not proof the party physically visited the site. Do not conflate separate children in Karrhu and Keldor.
- **Consequences replace sermons.** A guarded ford stays passable; a lost route stays lost; a letter travels onward; a relayed notice triggers a decision; a dangerous body passes close enough to force Brunti to move.
- **Mystery remains a lived event.** Blackwood gets less explanation, not more answers. Brunti's missing time, the Choir, Mire, Procession, Guardian and Silent Throne retain their order and unknowns.
- **Read-aloud rhythm:** preserve a range of chapter endings: friendship and song, bodily relief, weather, a real scholarly decision, a safe return deliberately refused, one impossible silence, a physical archive repair, and an ordinary meal. Shorten repeated abstract concluding sentences where the scene already closes.
