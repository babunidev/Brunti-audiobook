## R10 — two distinct lives, anonymous until the on-page inscriptions

[Ch1](../manuscript/chapters/01-skyland-morning.md) now makes two *visible tool styles* part of Brunti's original dangerous examination: fine, short chisel strokes above and wider square marks nearer the lower casing. The cover still conceals their origins; a single worker changing tools remains possible. [Ch2](../manuscript/chapters/02-the-weight-of-other-stones.md) gives Darrik's existing measurement page a copy of **those unnamed marks**. These are material traces available before the journey, not name knowledge or fresh testimony from the dead.

[Ch11](../manuscript/chapters/11-the-city-of-listening.md) now gives the Aetherion diagram **differently spaced tool/fastening marks on its two depicted compartments**. The document remains broken, its sender and occupants unknown, and the archive original stays with Velen. This foreshadows a possible two-part craft history without proving that the old drawing came from Brunti's particular wall.

At the **original Chapter 29 inscription reveal** ([Ch29](../manuscript/chapters/29-the-work-of-bringing-back.md)), the two material patterns can be compared with the **physically exposed** stones without silently attributing all cut marks to either deceased person. The independently witnessed names remain first revealed in this chapter:

- **Tavra**, the deceased Skykeeper: a contemporary-style account in the copied Skyland route index includes the **correction of a family's name**, preserving both the original spelling and the amendment. [Ch30](../manuscript/chapters/30-the-note-that-remains.md) gives an older memory-keeper an **explicitly qualified inherited anecdote** of a messenger returning to correct a person's name.
- **Korrum**, the deceased builder of lived-in spaces: a distinct old maintenance entry recalls a **landing widened for people who needed room to turn**, rather than a spectacular project. Chapter 30 keeps the ordinary loose-step story and gives the landing work a separate, useful human context.

These accounts are **novel-only fragments of lives**, not established MorkahjaWorld biographies and not proof that the memory stones contain thinking personalities. The first name is not revealed in Chapters 1–28; the second remains under an obscuring lower strip until Chapter 29. They are **not** parents of Brunti, divine emissaries or explanatory keys to Keldor/Blackwood. The documented archive fragments retain appropriate uncertainty rather than becoming perfect biographies.

## R12 — trust changes through specific work, not unconditional forgiveness

**Sira:** [Ch27](../manuscript/chapters/27-what-the-throne-keeps.md) already retains her justified anger at Brunti's needless Blackwood choice; the wound is not erased. In [Ch28](../manuscript/chapters/28-a-story-about-the-end.md), Sira explicitly asks for **Brunti's own account of the memorial repair**, distinct from the council report. She continues her work on missing family contacts, and the farewell leaves anger unresolved without forbidding future friendship. In [Ch30](../manuscript/chapters/30-the-note-that-remains.md), **Brunti follows through**: she sends Sira a properly carried letter naming what Darrik's crew achieved, what her injured wrist could and could not do, and that Blackwood contributed no memorial answer; she asks about the girl without assuming a reunion. **No reply or forgiveness is invented.**

**Velen:** Their evidence practices remain independent from Brunti's sensations. In [Ch29](../manuscript/chapters/29-the-work-of-bringing-back.md), Velen explicitly asks an older teller whether a remembered bridge story may be attributed; the account's disputed dimensions remain disputed. In [Ch30](../manuscript/chapters/30-the-note-that-remains.md), they distinguish the **publicly checkable inscription/restoration record** from Brunti's **private, attributed but unverified Blackwood narrative**, and agree not to circulate her pages without permission. They neither turn a witness into a source of objective facts about the abyss nor suppress her account.

**Darrik:** His safety plan remains correct and independently necessary. [Ch29](../manuscript/chapters/29-the-work-of-bringing-back.md) makes his **own decision** to move the copying tables and choose a public, accessible listening court follow from the elder's need to sit and the caretaker's need not to abandon the resting chamber; Brunti does not simply instruct him to learn a lesson. He supervises the protected opening, independent structural checks, replacement wall and safe transfer. [Ch30](../manuscript/chapters/30-the-note-that-remains.md) retains his final inspection standards and the next-day handrail/lamp work. Their reconciliation remains **shared measured labor**, not a claim that either was right about everything.

**Caretaker and harmed resident:** Chapter 29 now notes that the resident who suffered Brunti's early brace has resumed the shaped resting bed **but still expects warning before nearby work**. The caretaker supplied the work time; Brunti respects the specific request. The initial harm is not abstracted into an injury erased by the finale.

## R13 — mortality without a second divinity plot

The Atranor sequence in [Ch28](../manuscript/chapters/28-a-story-about-the-end.md) still comes from **Tarnel repeating his deceased sister's inherited story**, not from Brunti entering the divine realm. **No divine rule is changed:** resurrection, passage to a god's inner realm and freely chosen reincarnation remain distinct; the dead do not reappear as freshly speaking memory stones.

A small original interaction now follows Tarnel's story. His sister's **worn thimble case** catches at the lining; Brunti offers to fix the clasp **after asking**. Tarnel declines because the familiar motion of opening it is part of how he remembers her. The case remains his, small flaw included. This connects grief to the practical choice of when not to repair a cherished thing, without converting the Atranor testimony into a clue about Brunti's wall.
